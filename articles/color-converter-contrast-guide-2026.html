6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What are the required contrast ratios for WCAG 2.1 Level AA and AAA?

Level AA requires a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for normal body text and 3.0:1 for large text (at least 18pt or 14pt bold). Level AAA requires 7.0:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text.

Why is 0.05 added to both luminance values in the formula?

The 0.05 constant prevents division by zero when calculating contrast against pitch black (L = 0) and simulates the physiological effect of atmospheric light scatter and screen surface reflectance.

Does WCAG contrast apply to UI components and button borders?

Yes. Under WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.11 (Non-text Contrast), user interface components, input boundaries, focus indicators, and graphical icons must maintain at least a 3.0:1 contrast ratio against adjacent colors.

How does HSL differ from RGB and HEX in modern development?

While HEX and RGB describe color through raw hardware light intensities, HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) aligns with human psychological perception, making it trivial to dynamically compute complementary palettes, tints, and hover states.

Why do pure blue buttons with white text often cause eye strain?

Human eyes have significantly fewer blue cone photoreceptors in the fovea centralis. Highly saturated pure blues trigger chromatic aberration when paired with sharp white text, making high-contrast verification essential for readability.
